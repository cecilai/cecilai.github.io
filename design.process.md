Interview

I interviewed a friend who has been having difficulty balancing friendships with schoolwork, grades, and sports. We discussed how busy schedules make it harder to spend time with friends and how school responsibilities can get in the way of socializing. I learned that maintaining friendships is important to her, but finding time to hang out can be difficult.

Principles

The most important things to her were maintaining close friendships, having time to socialize, and balancing schoolwork with extracurricular activities.

Problem Space: How might we help students maintain close friendships while balancing busy academic and extracurricular schedules?

Raw Ideas

Buy tickets to a concert so friends have something exciting to look forward to together.

Buy candy to bribe friends into making time to hang out.

Set an alarm to remind friends to do their homework.

Buy friends In-N-Out and study together afterward.

Create a weekly friendship day dedicated to spending time together.

Create a friendship reminder bracelet.

Invent teleportation earrings that allow friends to instantly meet up.

Create a professional procrastination detector that identifies when someone is procrastinating instead of doing their work.

More-Complete Ideas: Gallery Sketches

Sketch 1: Friendship Reminder Bracelet

A bracelet that helps friends remember to stay connected, even when they are busy with school and other responsibilities.

Sketch 2: Shoes with Wheels

Shoes with wheels that make it easier and faster to travel between places and meet up with friends.

Sketch 3: In-N-Out Study Dates

Friends meet at In-N-Out after school to eat, study, and spend time together. This combines socializing with schoolwork instead of having to choose between the two.

Experiment & Path Forward 

I decided to experiment with the Friendship Beeping Bracelet. The idea is that the bracelets randomly beep at different times throughout the day, reminding friends of each other even when they are busy or apart. When someone hears their bracelet beep, they can think about their friend and maybe reach out to them. After Thanksgiving break, I want to explore how to design the bracelet and test whether these small reminders can help friends stay connected.

Reflections

Through this experience, I learned that design thinking starts with understanding someone's actual needs instead of immediately jumping to a solution. I found brainstorming interesting because I came up with a variety of ideas, including some that were unrealistic or funny. It helped me think outside the box and consider different ways to solve the same problem. I also learned that a solution does not always have to be complicated. Sometimes, a small reminder or change in someone's routine can make a difference. I want to take the importance of understanding people's needs before designing a solution with me in future projects.
