nterview

I interviewed a friend who has been having difficulty balancing friendships with schoolwork, grades, and sports. We discussed how busy schedules make it harder to spend time with friends, especially when there are assignments, tests, practices, and other responsibilities to keep up with. I learned that even though she still wants to spend time with her friends, it can be difficult to make plans when everyone has different schedules. Sometimes, people get so caught up in their daily responsibilities that they forget to make time for their friendships. This helped me better understand the problem and think about ways to make staying connected easier.

Principles

The most important things to her were maintaining close friendships, having time to socialize, and balancing schoolwork with extracurricular activities. She wanted to be able to spend time with friends without feeling like she was falling behind in school or sacrificing other responsibilities. I realized that the solution should make maintaining friendships easier rather than adding another responsibility to an already busy schedule.

Problem Space: How might we help students maintain close friendships while balancing busy academic and extracurricular schedules?

Raw Ideas

Buy tickets to a concert so friends have something exciting to look forward to together.

Buy candy to bribe friends into making time to hang out.

Set an alarm to remind friends to do their homework.

Buy friends In-N-Out and study together afterward.

Create a weekly friendship day dedicated to spending time together.

Create a friendship reminder bracelet.

Invent teleportation earrings that allow friends to instantly meet up.

Create a professional procrastination detector that identifies when someone is procrastinating instead of doing their work.

Create a friendship calendar that automatically finds free time in everyone's schedules.

Make a friendship countdown clock that counts down to the next time friends can hang out.

Experiment & Path Forward

I decided to experiment with the Friendship Beeping Bracelet. The idea is that two friends each have a bracelet that randomly beeps at different times throughout the day. The beeping acts as a small reminder of the other person, even when they are busy with school, sports, or other activities. They do not have to press anything or actively send a notification because the bracelet does it automatically. When someone hears their bracelet beep, it gives them a moment to think about their friend and possibly reach out to them.

After Thanksgiving break, I want to explore how the bracelet could actually be designed and what materials or technology would be needed to make it work. I also want to test whether the random reminders would help friends feel more connected or encourage them to reach out to each other more often.

Reflections

Through this experience, I learned that design thinking starts with understanding someone's actual needs instead of immediately jumping to a solution. Interviewing someone helped me see the problem from another person's perspective rather than just assuming what they needed. I found the brainstorming process especially interesting because I came up with a variety of ideas, including some that were unrealistic or funny. Even though not every idea would work in real life, they helped me think outside the box and consider different approaches to the same problem.

I also realized that a solution does not always have to be complicated or completely change someone's routine. Sometimes, a small reminder or simple interaction can make a difference. My favorite part was developing the Friendship Beeping Bracelet because it takes an everyday object and gives it a purpose beyond just wearing it. I want to take the importance of listening to people, experimenting with different ideas, and being open to unexpected solutions with me in future projects.
